# Lesson: What Changes and Why
hero: guide-thinking
reflection: What has changed most for you, or what do you expect to change most, when a child joins your life?
takeaway: Lower desire after a child arrives is common, has real causes, and usually shifts again with time and care.

## Every Path to Parenthood
art: guide-study-together
plain: Birth, adoption, fostering, surrogacy or joining a family with kids all change intimacy.

People become parents in many ways: giving birth, a partner giving birth, adoption, fostering, surrogacy, or joining a family that already has children. Every path brings a new person who needs a lot of time and attention.

This lesson is for all parents, in any relationship structure. Some parts, like healing after birth, apply only to some people.

## Why Desire Often Drops
art: guide-sleepy
plain: Exhaustion, being touched all day and a new identity can all quiet desire for a while.

Common reasons desire dips after a child arrives:

- **Sleep loss.** Tired bodies put rest ahead of sex.
- **Feeling "touched out".** After hours of holding and feeding a baby, more touch can feel like too much.
- **A new identity.** Many parents need time to feel like a sexual person again, not only a parent.
- **Hormones.** After giving birth, and while breastfeeding or chestfeeding, hormone changes can lower desire and cause vaginal dryness.

None of this means something is wrong with you or your relationship.

> Many parents feel "touched out" after a day of caregiving. It is a normal response, not a sign of a failing relationship.

## Desire Can Come Back Differently
art: guide-encourage
plain: For many parents, desire shows up after closeness starts rather than before.

For many parents, desire becomes more responsive: it shows up after closeness begins instead of before. That is a normal way to feel desire, not a lesser one. (See Understanding Desire for more on responsive desire.)

# Lesson: After Birth: Healing and Body Changes
hero: guide-meditate
reflection: What would help you feel ready, physically and emotionally, to be sexual again after a birth?
takeaway: Wait for your clinician's OK, go slowly, and never push through pain.

## Wait for the Okay
art: guide-lesson-reading
plain: Ask your clinician when it is safe to have sex again. There is no deadline after that.

After giving birth, the body needs time to heal. Ask your doctor or midwife when it is safe to have sex again. Many people are checked a few weeks after birth, but everyone heals at their own pace.

Getting the OK is permission, not a deadline. Start when you feel ready.

## Go Slowly and Use Lube
art: guide-thinking-b
plain: Start gently, use plenty of lubricant, and stop if anything hurts.

- Start with touch you both enjoy, without a goal.
- Use plenty of lubricant, especially while breastfeeding or chestfeeding, when dryness is common.
- Stop if something hurts. **Pain is a signal, not something to push through.**

Ongoing pain is worth raising with your clinician. A pelvic floor physical therapist can help with pain, leaking and recovery.

check: Sex after birth is painful for Jo. What does the lesson suggest?
- Keep going so it gets easier
- Avoid mentioning it so the partner doesn't worry
- *Stop, and talk to a clinician or pelvic floor physical therapist
> Pain is a signal to stop. Clinicians and pelvic floor physical therapists can help.

## Plan for Contraception
art: guide-thinking-c
plain: You can get pregnant again before your period returns.

It is possible to get pregnant again before periods return, even while breastfeeding or chestfeeding. If you do not want another pregnancy yet, talk about contraception with your clinician before you start having sex again.

## Mental Health Matters Too
art: guide-encourage-c
plain: Depression and anxiety after a new child are common in any parent and very treatable.

Depression and anxiety after a new child are common, can affect any parent (not only the one who gave birth), and are very treatable. Signs include feeling hopeless, numb, constantly anxious, or unable to enjoy anything for more than two weeks.

In the US, the National Maternal Mental Health Hotline is free and open any time: call or text 1-833-852-6262. If you think about harming yourself or your baby, call 988 or 911 right away.

> Depression after a new child can affect any parent, and it is very treatable. Reaching out is the right step.

# Lesson: Staying Partners, Not Just Co-Parents
hero: guide-high-five
reflection: What is one small way you could show your partner you still see them as a partner, not only a co-parent?
takeaway: Fair sharing of the work and small daily moments of connection protect intimacy.

## Share the Load Fairly
art: guide-study-together
plain: Unfair division of chores and planning is one of the biggest desire killers.

Desire often fades when one person carries most of the work, including the planning and remembering (sometimes called the mental load). It is hard to feel close to someone you feel like you are managing.

Sit down together and list everything the household needs, from night feeds to booking appointments. Decide together who owns what, and check in every few weeks.

check: Maya does most of the night feeds and all the planning, and her desire has dropped. What might help most?
- Scheduling more date nights while keeping the work the same
- *Sharing the work, including the planning, more fairly
- Waiting until the baby is older
> When one partner carries most of the load, desire often drops. Sharing the work, including the planning, tends to help.

## Small, Daily Connection
art: guide-high-five
plain: Short moments of attention each day matter more than one big date.

Short moments add up: a real hug, a few minutes of talking about something other than the baby, a thank-you for something specific. (See Maintaining Long-term Intimacy for more ideas.)

## Touch Without Pressure
art: guide-meditate-b
plain: Cuddling and affection that don't have to lead to sex rebuild closeness.

Agree that some touch is just touch, with no expectation that it leads to sex. When affection does not come with pressure, many parents find they want more of it.

## Planning Isn't Unromantic
art: guide-daily-spin
plain: With a child at home, planned time together is often the only time there is.

With a child at home, spontaneous time is rare. Planning time together is not a sign that the spark is gone. It is how busy parents make room for each other.

# Lesson: Talking About Sex and Getting Support
hero: guide-story-chat
reflection: What would you want a partner to know about how your desire has changed?
takeaway: Talk with curiosity, not blame, and get help if you feel stuck.

## Talk About It Without Blame
art: guide-story-chat-c
plain: Share what you feel and need, and ask about your partner's experience too.

Try "I" statements: "I miss feeling close to you" works better than "You never want sex anymore." Ask your partner how they are experiencing this time too. You are on the same side.

check: Which opening is most likely to help?
- "You never want sex anymore."
- "Is something wrong with you?"
- *"I miss feeling close to you. How are you feeling about us lately?"
> Sharing your own feeling and asking about theirs invites a conversation instead of a defense.

## Different Levels of Desire
art: guide-thinking
plain: Differences in desire are normal and can be worked through together.

It is very common for partners' desire to differ after a child arrives. Neither person is wrong. Talk about what each of you needs, look for kinds of intimacy that work for both of you, and revisit it as things change.

If your family includes more than two adults, include everyone affected in these talks and in planning time together.

## When to Get Help
art: guide-encourage
plain: If you feel stuck or resentful for months, a therapist can help.

If you feel stuck, resentful or distant for months, a couples therapist or sex therapist can help. (See Common Sexual Concerns for finding the right professional.)
