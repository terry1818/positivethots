# Lesson: Before You Meet
hero: guide-thinking
reflection: What is one safety step you skipped in the past that you want to make a habit?
takeaway: A little checking before you meet keeps the fun part fun.

## Why a Little Checking Helps
art: guide-lesson-reading
plain: Most people are who they say they are. A few quick checks protect you from the few who are not.

Meeting someone new is exciting. Most people you talk to are exactly who they say they are. A few are not, and the checks below take only a few minutes.

Checking is not rude and it is not a sign of distrust. People who mean well usually expect it and often do the same checks on you.

## Make Sure They Are Real
art: guide-study-together
plain: A short video call is the quickest way to know the person matches their photos.

- **Have a short video call first.** A few minutes on camera confirms the person matches their photos and gives you a feel for them.
- **Look for a consistent story.** Name, age, work and where they live should line up across what they tell you.
- **Search their photos.** A reverse image search shows whether the pictures belong to someone else.
- **Notice their verification.** A verified profile is a good sign, not a guarantee.

If someone always has a reason not to show their face on camera, take that seriously.

## Warning Signs in Messages
art: guide-thinking-b
plain: Rushing you, refusing video, or any talk of money are reasons to slow down or stop.

Slow down or stop if someone:

- pushes to move the conversation off the app very quickly
- refuses a video call again and again
- tells a dramatic story and then asks for money, gift cards or crypto
- pressures you for photos, your home address or where you work
- gets angry when you set a limit or take time to reply

**Never send money to someone you have not met in person.** Asking for money is the most common sign of a scam.

check: Your new connection won't video call but asks for a loan for a plane ticket to meet you. What fits best?
- Send a small amount to show good faith
- Send it if they promise to pay you back
- *Stop and don't send money; this matches a common scam
> Refusing video and then asking for money is the classic pattern of a romance scam. Do not send money to someone you have not met.

## Trust Your Gut
art: guide-encourage
plain: You do not need proof to say no. A bad feeling is enough of a reason.

You do not need evidence to step back. If something feels off, you can pause, ask more questions, or stop talking. A person who respects you will respect that.

check: You feel uneasy about someone but can't say why. What does this lesson suggest?
- *Treat the feeling as a reason to slow down or stop
- Ignore it unless you can prove something is wrong
- Meet anyway so you don't seem rude
> Your unease is information. You can slow down or step away without proving anything.

# Lesson: Planning the First Meeting
hero: guide-story-chat
reflection: Who would you tell about a first meeting, and what would you share with them?
takeaway: Public place, your own way there and back, and a friend who knows the plan.

## Pick a Public Place
art: guide-waving
plain: Meet somewhere busy, like a café or a park in daytime, not at anyone's home.

Choose a busy public place such as a café, a lively bar or a park during the day. Avoid meeting at either person's home, or anywhere isolated, until you know each other better.

A short first meeting, like a coffee or a walk, takes pressure off both of you. You can always plan a longer second meeting.

## Get There and Back on Your Own
art: guide-practice-dumbbell
plain: Use your own transport so you can leave whenever you want.

Arrange your own way there and back: drive, take transit or book your own ride. That way you can leave whenever you want and you do not have to share your address.

Keep your phone charged and bring a little money in case plans change.

## Tell a Friend the Plan
art: guide-study-together
plain: Share who, where and when with someone you trust, and agree a time to check in.

Before you go, tell a friend:

- who you are meeting (their name and a photo or profile)
- where and when you are meeting
- when you will message them to say you are OK

Agree what your friend will do if they do not hear from you. Sharing your live location for the evening is another option.

check: Which plan keeps you most in control on a first meeting?
- They pick you up from home so you don't have to drive
- You meet at their place because it's quieter
- *You meet at a café, travel there yourself, and a friend knows the plan
> Your own transport, a public place and a friend who knows the plan mean you can leave at any time and someone knows where you are.

## Keep Some Things Private for Now
art: guide-thinking-c
plain: Your home address, workplace and money details can wait until trust is built.

There is no rush to share your home address, exact workplace, or financial details. Trust builds over time, and it is fine to keep those for later.

# Lesson: During the Meeting
hero: guide-high-five
reflection: What would you say to leave a meeting that isn't going well?
takeaway: You can leave at any point, for any reason.

## Your Drink, Your Pace
art: guide-thinking
plain: Keep your drink in sight and drink at a pace that keeps you clear-headed.

- Order your own drinks and keep them in sight.
- If you leave a drink unattended, get a fresh one.
- Go at a pace that keeps you clear-headed. Having little or nothing to drink is always fine.

If you suddenly feel much drunker than you should, or unwell, tell staff or someone you trust straight away and get help.

## Check In With Yourself
art: guide-meditate
plain: Notice how you feel during the meeting, not just how they feel about you.

Every so often, notice how you are feeling. Relaxed and curious? Or tense, pressured or unsure?

Consent applies from the first meeting: either of you can change your mind about anything at any time. (See Consent & Sexual Autonomy for more.)

## It Is OK to Leave
art: guide-waving-b
plain: You never owe anyone more of your time. A short, polite line is enough.

You can end a meeting at any point. You do not need a big reason. Simple lines work:

- "Thanks for meeting up. I'm going to head home now."
- "I don't think we're a fit, but it was nice to meet you."

If someone ignores your no, follows you, or keeps pushing, go to staff or a busy area and get help. In an emergency in the US, call 911.

check: Halfway through a coffee, Sam feels pressured and wants to go. What should Sam do?
- *Say a short goodbye and leave; no big reason is needed
- Stay until the end so it isn't awkward
- Agree to go somewhere else to smooth things over
> You never owe anyone more time. A short, polite goodbye is enough.

## Signs of Pressure
art: guide-thinking-b
plain: Pushing past a no, guilt trips and rushing you somewhere private are signs to leave.

Leave, or get help, if the other person:

- keeps pushing after you say no
- tries to guilt you ("I came all this way…")
- tries to move you somewhere private quickly
- makes comments that leave you feeling unsafe

check: Your date says "I came all this way" when you say you'd like to end the evening. What is this?
- A fair reason to stay longer
- *A guilt trip, which is a sign of pressure
- A normal part of flirting
> Using guilt to get you to stay is a form of pressure. You can still leave.

# Lesson: After the Meeting
hero: guide-encourage
reflection: How do you like to tell someone you aren't interested, kindly and clearly?
takeaway: Check in, be honest, and use Block and Report when you need them.

## Check In With Your Friend
art: guide-story-chat-c
plain: Message your friend as agreed so they know you are safe.

Send the message you agreed with your friend, even if the evening went well. It closes the loop and means they will not worry.

## Be Honest About What's Next
art: guide-high-five
plain: Say whether you'd like to meet again. A kind, clear no is better than silence.

If you would like to meet again, say so. If not, a short, kind message is better than disappearing:

"Thanks for meeting me. I enjoyed chatting, but I didn't feel the connection I'm looking for. Wishing you the best."

You do not owe a long explanation. If someone reacts badly to a polite no, that tells you something.

check: You don't want to see someone again. Which is the kindest clear option?
- Stop replying and hope they get the message
- Keep chatting so they don't feel bad
- *Send a short, polite message saying you're not interested
> A short, honest message is clearer and kinder than silence, and you do not owe a long explanation.

## Block and Report
art: guide-encourage-c
plain: If someone makes you feel unsafe, block them and report them so we can protect others.

If someone pressures you, lies about who they are, asks for money, or makes you feel unsafe, you can block them and report them from your chat. Reports help keep everyone in the community safe, and you do not need to be sure before you report.

## If Something Happened
art: guide-meditate-b
plain: What happened is not your fault. Free, private help is available any time.

If something happened that you did not want, it is not your fault. You can get free, confidential support from RAINN's National Sexual Assault Hotline at 1-800-656-4673, any time. In an emergency in the US, call 911.

You decide what to do next, including whether to report to the police. A trusted friend, a doctor or a support line can help you think it through.

check: Someone pressured Priya on a date. What does this lesson say?
- It was partly her fault for meeting them
- *It is not her fault, and free, confidential help is available
- She should wait to see if it happens again before telling anyone
> Pressure or harm is the responsibility of the person who did it. Support is available any time.
