# Lesson: Before You Go
hero: guide-thinking
reflection: What would you want to agree with a partner before going to an event together?
takeaway: Read the rules, make your plans and agreements ahead of time, and arrive clear-headed.

## Read the Event's Rules
art: guide-lesson-reading
plain: Every event has its own rules. Read them before you go and follow them there.

Events range from social mixers to sex-positive parties, play parties, kink events and festivals. Each has its own rules about dress, consent, photos, alcohol and what can happen where.

Read the code of conduct before you go. If something is unclear, ask the host. Following the rules is part of the deal of being invited.

## Talk With Partners First
art: guide-story-chat
plain: Agree beforehand what each of you is and isn't open to, and how you'll check in.

If you are going with a partner or partners, talk before the event, not in the middle of it:

- What is each person open to, and what is off the table?
- How will you check in during the night?
- What is your safer sex plan? (See Sexual Health & Harm Reduction.)
- What happens if one of you wants to leave?

check: Two partners disagree in the middle of a party about what's OK. What would have helped most?
- Deciding on the spot without the other partner
- Avoiding parties entirely
- *Agreeing on limits and check-ins before going
> Talking about limits and check-ins beforehand prevents hard negotiations in a busy, distracting place.

## Plan Your Night
art: guide-practice-dumbbell
plain: Arrange your way home, bring what you need, and keep alcohol low or none.

Arrange your own way home, bring any supplies you need, and keep alcohol and other substances low or skip them. You need a clear head to give and receive consent.

# Lesson: Consent in a Crowd
hero: guide-high-five
reflection: How would you like someone to ask you before touching you?
takeaway: Ask every person, every time, and take any no or hesitation as a no.

## Ask Before Any Touch
art: guide-thinking-b
plain: Always ask before touching anyone, even a hug, and even if you've touched before.

Ask before touching anyone, even for a hug, even if you have touched before. Being at an event, wearing certain clothes or flirting is not consent. (See Consent & Sexual Autonomy.)

## Ask Each Person Separately
art: guide-study-together
plain: When approaching a couple or group, every person decides for themselves.

When you approach a couple or a group, every person gives their own answer. One partner cannot say yes for another.

check: Taylor asks a couple to join them, and one partner says "Sure, we're both in." What should Taylor do?
- *Ask the other partner directly
- Go ahead, since one partner agreed for both
- Leave without saying anything
> Each person consents for themselves. Taylor should hear a yes from the other partner too.

## No Means No, and Maybe Means No
art: guide-thinking-c
plain: Accept a no, a maybe or silence the first time, without arguing or asking again later.

Take a no, a maybe, a pause or silence as a no. Accept it gracefully the first time. Do not argue, sulk or ask again later in the night.

Someone who is very drunk or high cannot give consent.

## Watching Needs Consent Too
art: guide-thinking
plain: Ask before joining or watching a scene, and follow the event's rules about watching.

At some events, people play in shared spaces. Follow the event's rules about watching, keep a respectful distance, and never join or interrupt without asking first.

check: You see a scene you'd like to join. What is the right move?
- Join in quietly so you don't interrupt
- Stand very close to get noticed
- *Wait for a natural pause and ask, or don't join at all
> Joining without asking breaks consent. Ask at an appropriate moment, and accept the answer.

# Lesson: Privacy and Respect
hero: guide-meditate
reflection: What would you need to feel safe being yourself at an event?
takeaway: No photos, no outing anyone, and treat everyone as you'd want to be treated.

## No Photos Without Permission
art: guide-lesson-reading-b
plain: Never take or share photos or videos of people without their clear permission.

Never photograph or film anyone without clear permission, and follow the event's photo rules. Many events ban phones in certain areas.

## Don't Out Anyone
art: guide-meditate-b
plain: Who you saw and what they did stays private. Seeing someone there doesn't give you the right to share it.

Do not tell anyone who you saw at an event or what they did. Some people are not open about their relationships, sexuality or interests at work or with family. Outing someone can cost them their job, relationships or safety.

check: Sam sees a coworker at an event. What should Sam do?
- Mention it at work so things aren't awkward
- *Keep it private and follow the coworker's lead
- Tell a few trusted friends
> Who attends an event is private. Sam should keep it to themselves.

## Respect Everyone's Space
art: guide-encourage
plain: Use the names people give, don't hover, and welcome newcomers.

- Use the names and pronouns people give you.
- Do not hover around people who are busy or who have said no.
- Be kind to newcomers. Everyone was new once.
- Respect hosts and volunteers. They make the event possible.

# Lesson: When Something Goes Wrong
hero: guide-encourage-c
reflection: Who would you tell if you saw something worrying at an event?
takeaway: Speak up to the hosts, look out for each other, and check in after.

## Tell the Hosts
art: guide-study-together
plain: If someone breaks consent or makes you uneasy, tell a host or the consent team.

If someone breaks consent, ignores a no, or makes you or others feel unsafe, tell a host, a consent volunteer or staff right away. Reporting helps keep everyone safe. You do not need to be sure.

check: You notice someone ignoring another guest's no. What should you do?
- Mind your own business
- *Tell a host or the consent team
- Post about it online later
> Hosts and consent teams can act straight away. Telling them protects everyone.

## Look Out for Each Other
art: guide-high-five
plain: Check on friends who seem unwell or overwhelmed, and get help if needed.

If someone seems very drunk, unwell or overwhelmed, check on them and get a host. In an emergency in the US, call 911.

## Leaving Early Is Fine
art: guide-waving-b
plain: You can leave at any time, for any reason, and you don't owe an explanation.

You can leave at any time, for any reason. If you came with partners, follow the plan you agreed for leaving, or send a message so no one worries.

## Check In Afterward
art: guide-story-chat-c
plain: Talk with partners the next day about what went well and what to change.

A day or two later, talk with partners about what went well, what felt hard, and what you would change next time. Big nights can bring up big feelings, so make room for care afterward.

check: After a party, one partner feels unsettled. What helps most?
- Ignore it, since the party is over
- Blame the other partner for going
- *Talk it through with care and decide what to change next time
> A caring check-in after an event helps partners process feelings and improve next time.
